A Measured Five-Layer RTOS Architecture for Deterministic Flight Control on ESP32-S3

Quy Hao Nhien Nguyen1,2, Gia-Huy Luu1,2, Cat Gia Han Nguyen1,2, Ngo Dai Cat Truong1,2, Tuan Nguyen Lam1,2

1 Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, Ho Chi Minh City University of Technology (HCMUT), Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
2 Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City, Ho Chi Minh City 70000, Vietnam

INTRODUCTION

  • Context. Hard real-time flight control requires data acquisition, preprocessing, state estimation and control to execute at exact periods; a single late step corrupts the entire chain.
  • Verified gap. Within the PX4 ecosystem, the ESP32 appears solely as a Wi-Fi telemetry module, and the autopilot catalogue contains no ESP32 board; the ArduPilot ESP32-S3 port remains officially experimental; Espressif's ESP-Drone has received limited support since 12/2022. Feasibility has therefore already been demonstrated by the community.
  • Claim. The remaining gap is measured determinism combined with a verifiable architectural ruleset — which constitutes the contribution of this work.

Verified gap sources: PX4 documentation (Wi-Fi telemetry module, autopilot hardware list), ArduPilot ESP32 port documentation, esp-drone README.

METHODOLOGY

2.1 Hardware

  • ESP32-S3, dual-core, 240 MHz; BLE signalling / telemetry link; native 128-bit PIE SIMD used through the L1 compute HAL.
  • GY-87 sensor module: MPU6050 (accelerometer + gyroscope), HMC5883L (magnetometer), BMP180 (barometer).
  • MicroSD blackbox logging; WS2812B status LEDs; LoRa footprint; companion power-monitor board.
  • Carrier: self-designed 4-layer KiCad PCB (FC_v01; FC_v02 revision in progress), gerbers and BOM released with the source repository.

Design intent: the cheapest available breakout sensors on a purpose-designed carrier — a cost-optimised sensor set still supporting a deterministic, verifiable stack.

2.2 Firmware

The system is a five-layer architecture — L5 Application (FSM) → L4 Flight Core & Algorithms → L3 Middleware & IPC → L2 Drivers → L1 Platform HAL — governed by one rule: dependencies point downward only, so no lower layer imports a higher one and no target-specific type leaks into an L2–L4 public header; the invariants are kept checkable through recorded design decisions.

Compute-heavy kernels (EKF prediction/update, ellipsoid calibration fits, filtering) reach the S3 SIMD unit through hal_simd / hal_matrix, which map dot, matrix-multiply and 3×3 inverse onto ESP-DSP dsps_* / dspm_* routines with zero dynamic allocation.

2.3 Experimental setup

The majority of the experiments are static analyses, performed prior to any flight attempt:

  • Sensors: time-domain histograms → per-axis standard deviation; FFT of the raw streams → noise floor and periodic interference bands.
  • Task frequency and jitter: nominal periods from config.h against true periods from SystemView (JTAG/TRAX, not SD) — median / p99 / max per task, ≥10⁴ loops, full-system load with RF active.
  • Logging robustness: BLE and SD overflow / drop counters, BLE MTU validation, SD fsync latency peaks against ring-buffer depth.
  • Estimators: attitude EKF (7-state quaternion + gyro bias; 500 Hz roll/pitch, 100 Hz yaw, unit-sphere mag fusion) and altitude EKF (3-state, baro + accel, 100 Hz).
  • IMU calibration: gyro static offset; accelerometer 6-DOF and magnetometer 9-DOF ellipsoid fits, SIMD-accelerated, no dynamic RAM.

CONCLUSION AND CONTRIBUTIONS

  1. A five-layer RTOS architecture for an ESP32-S3 flight controller with measured determinism (SystemView), 500 Hz acquisition and EKF.
  2. Zero-dynamic-allocation design and zero-RAM SIMD calibration — a technique set for RAM-constrained MCUs.
  3. An evidence-driven development process based on recorded design decisions, reusable for other embedded projects.
  4. Self-designed flight-controller hardware: 4-layer carrier PCB (FC_v01 → FC_v02) with a companion power-monitor board; gerbers and BOM released.

Future work: INDI control law, mixer, barometer upgrade or ToF + optical flow (altitude-hold path), free flight, and full-system + RF re-profiling.

DISCUSSION

Cause of the altitude-EKF performance deficit. The BMP180 trades resolution, output rate and noise level for cost, and the estimator cannot recover information that the sensor does not provide. The improvement path is a higher-resolution, higher-rate digital barometer, or time-of-flight (ToF) and optical-flow sensing for direct, low-latency observation of height and vertical velocity — scheduled as FC_v02 hardware work.

System-level assessment. The design is evaluated as a whole rather than through any single artefact: the layered architecture separates sensing, estimation and platform concerns so that each can be validated independently; real-time behaviour is quantified rather than assumed (SystemView jitter statistics over ≥10⁴ loops under full-system load); and the sensing chain is deliberately cost-optimised, with its limits measured and reported openly. Taken together, these results indicate that a low-cost, self-designed flight-controller stack can meet the timing discipline that flight demands — supporting the conclusion that the system is feasible for deployment on and flight with a real drone.

Positioning relative to related work. No platform in the reviewed set publishes a full-system determinism trace together with an architectural ruleset; the closest work (arXiv 2607.23220) claims “jitter-free” operation without a profiler trace, and the ESP32 studies surveyed report timing benchmarks, flight without measurement, or documented failure to fly.

Remaining gaps: realtime altitude measurement, full-system + RF re-profiling, and the first free-flight validation.
